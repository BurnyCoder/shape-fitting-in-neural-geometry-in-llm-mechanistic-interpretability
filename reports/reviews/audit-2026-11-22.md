# Independent audit of 2026-11-22 (reviewer report)

**Scope.** I checked whether `main_s0`'s results are scientifically sound, and whether any methodological error invalidates them. I checked that the hypotheses are evaluated as preregistered, and that methods and definitions are used as their fields use them. I fact-checked the claims in README, reports, docs, paper and code comments against the stored results and the cited sources. This audit was done independently of the earlier ones: their conclusions were not taken as given. No model was trained or run. Every check reads stored results files, or is pure geometry on the shape families.

## Summary of the submission
- **Synthetic data.** A Set Transformer trained only on synthetic shapes classifies a top-3 PCA point cloud into 13 shape families and regresses canonical parameters.
- **H1, H2 (supported).** On 39,000 synthetic test clouds it beats a classical fit-every-family pipeline with geometric-AIC selection (B1). It also beats B1's post-hoc repair, B1-fixed.
- **H3 (not supported).** On 12 preregistered real activation datasets, its coarse shape class agrees with published descriptions on 7. That is not above a label-permutation null, nor above a constant "curve" answer.
- **H4 (supported, with qualifications).** Matched Gaussians are mostly labelled blob. Smooth ordinal processes are often labelled helix.

## Verdict
**No error found invalidates a reported verdict.** H1, H2 and H5b stay supported. H3 stays not supported, and H4 stays supported under the implemented (pooled) criterion. Five findings change how the results should be read:
- the absolute Chamfer values of surface families;
- the pooled H5b effect;
- the width of the confidence intervals;
- the wording of H4;
- two descriptions of real clouds.

All are fixed in the text or documented with new, script-generated numbers. One more limitation, the 2000-replicate resolution of the H1 p-value, is documented but not changed.

## What was verified (no change needed)

   - the method on the baseline subset;
   - H3: 7/12, Monte-Carlo permutation p ≈ 0.125 with 200,000 permutations, and the Clopper–Pearson CI.

   All match `analysis.json`.
2. **Test-set design.** These hold for all 1,560 chunks (`audit_2026-11-22.json` → `synthetic._design`):
   - N, the embedding draw and the family are constant within every generator chunk;
   - no (system, cloud) row is duplicated;
   - the true family is identical across systems for every cloud.

   The cluster ids used by the bootstrap are therefore correct, and every comparison is properly paired.
3. **Seed streams.**
   - Train, val and test come from disjoint seed bands (`seeding.py`).
   - B2's training data come from the train stream.
   - The temperature and B1-fixed's τ are fitted on val only.
   - No test data enter any fitted quantity.
4. **Statistics.**
   - The Holm step-down (Holm 1979) is implemented as defined.
   - ECE uses 15 bins on the top-1 prediction (Guo et al. 2017, Eq. 3).
   - Temperature scaling fits a single T by NLL on val.
   - The calibration code's reconstruction `T log p` gives the logits up to a per-sample constant, which softmax ignores.
   - The macro-F1 implementation equals sklearn over the true classes.
5. **Geometric AIC.**
   - AIC_G = J + 2(dN + p)ε², with ε² estimated from the most general model as J/((n − d)N − p), is Kanatani's criterion (Kanatani 2004, TPAMI 26(10)).
   - The proof that a d = 3 blob can never win holds, also when the degrees of freedom are clamped. With N = 5, a surface and p_blob = 9, the difference is J_blob + 11 J_g > 0.
6. **Matched-Gaussian control.** μ + g Xc/√N with g ~ N(0, I_N) has covariance Xc^T Xc / N, so the control matches the dataset's mean and covariance.
7. **Methodology claims that match the code:**
   - the label-margin table (`margin_ok` in every family);
   - the batch rule max(64, min(512, 262144 // N));
   - the cosine schedule to 5%;
   - the 61 refinement runs leaving up to 22 candidates per cloud: 4 blob + 5 cluster + 12 polytope + 10 × 4 other runs, giving 1 + 5 + 6 + 10 candidates;
   - the skipped position 0 in the counting extraction;
   - the layer convention.
8. **Claims about per-bin and per-family results** (`analysis.json`):
   - The method beats B1 in every N-bin, and the accuracy gap grows with N.
   - B2 is below B1 for 5–12 points and above it for N ≥ 13.
   - B1-fixed is below B1 for 5–12 points and above it for 257–1024.
   - Cone, helix and paraboloid have the lowest recall.
   - The largest confusions are helix↔cone, paraboloid↔cone and sphere↔cone, but most helix and paraboloid errors go elsewhere.
   - Fewer than half of the errors stay within the right coarse class.
9. **Descriptions of real clouds**, checked against the stored top-3 coordinates (`audit_2026-11-22.json` → `real_claims`):
   - The 6-feature toy model is three orthogonal antipodal pairs, an octahedron: off-diagonal cosines are −1 or |c| ≤ 0.002.
   - Weekday and month activations, and the random-init Llama controls, form one tight cluster per token. The between/within RMS ratio is 7.5–28.

## Findings

### F1. The Chamfer metric has a grid floor for surfaces (moderate; measurement validity)
**What.** The metric compares the fixed dense samples of the two shapes (`EVAL_DENSE`): 256 per curve, a 24 × 24 grid per surface, 256 Gaussian draws per blob. A geometrically perfect prediction therefore need not score 0.

**Measured** (`posthoc.design_checks.chamfer_floor`; `eval/metrics.py`, `chamfer_floor`):
- **Evaluation sampling vs a 16× denser sampling of the same shape:** at most about 0.01 for curves, about 0.09–0.10 for surfaces.
- **Two equivalent parameterisations of a sphere, cone or torus (rotated about the axis):** up to about 0.08.

The method's overall median Chamfer is 0.156, so absolute values of surface families partly measure the grid.

**Does it bias H2?** We expect not. The floor enters every system's score. The rotation about a symmetry axis cannot be inferred from the points, so neither the network nor B1 can align with the ground truth's grid. The floor should add noise, not favour one system.

**Fix.**
- Documented in the paper (table caption; limitations), the methodology, exp01 learning 13, the headline and the deviations log.
- A finer evaluation grid needs a re-evaluation with the model. That was out of scope (no inference).

### F2. The pooled H5b estimate hides opposite effects (moderate; interpretation)
**What.** H5b is the median-Chamfer difference between the method and no refinement, pooled over all families: −0.0033. Split by the true family's coarse class (`posthoc.design_checks.refinement_by_coarse`), refinement changes the median Chamfer by:

| Coarse class | Change in median Chamfer [95% CI] |
|---|---|
| Point sets | −0.009 [−0.018, −0.001] |
| Curves | −0.011 [−0.014, −0.008] |
| Surfaces | −0.010 [−0.012, −0.008] |
| Blobs | **+0.033** [0.025, 0.043] |

**Reading.** For curves, where the grid floor is negligible, refinement is a real gain in shape recovery. For blobs it is a loss. The text had described refinement as helping "only slightly" overall.

**Likely cause (untested).** The two-sided point-to-sample objective is not a covariance estimate. It moves the blob's fixed set of Gaussian draws toward the noisy points.

**Fix.** The paper's results, exp01 learning 8, the headline and deviations now report the split as post hoc. The preregistered pooled verdict is unchanged.

### F3. The cluster-bootstrap CIs are conservative (minor; statistics)
**What.** The test design fixes the family × N-bin × noise-bin cells and the number of chunks per cell: 10 in the full test set, 1 in the baseline subset. The reported cluster bootstrap resamples chunks across cells. It therefore treats the family mix as random and adds the spread between cell means to the variance.

**Why it is conservative.** This is the collapsed-strata variance estimator. Its design bias is a non-negative sum of squared differences of stratum means (Mosaferi 2015, [arXiv:1910.05840](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05840), Sec. 3.1; the method goes back to Hansen, Hurwitz & Madow 1953).

**Measured** (`posthoc.design_checks.stratified_ci`): a stratified cluster bootstrap, resampling chunks within their cell, narrows the method's full-test-set CIs:

| Statistic | Reported CI | Stratified CI |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | [86.9, 88.9] | [87.4, 88.3] |
| Median Chamfer | [0.148, 0.164] | [0.154, 0.158] |
| H5b | [−0.0055, −0.0011] | [−0.0048, −0.0020] |

On the baseline subset (one chunk per cell) a within-cell interval cannot be computed. H1 and H2 keep the conservative intervals, which already exclude 0 by a wide margin.

**Fix.** A `strata` option in `eval/stats.py` (unit-tested), new numbers in the headline and macros, and text in the paper's statistics paragraph, the limitations, the methodology and deviations.

### F4. The H1 p-value is a normal-approximation extrapolation (minor; disclosed)
**What.** H1's p-value comes from z = estimate / bootstrap SE (z ≈ 21, raw p ~ 1e-101). Its tail goes far beyond what 2000 bootstrap replicates resolve directly. Still, the CI lower bound (0.34) is about 19 SEs from 0, so no replicate can plausibly have been ≤ 0.

**Status.** The paper and methodology already say "normal approximation" and display a floor of "< 1e-16". No change. Readers should treat it as "p far below any conventional threshold", not as an exact tail probability.

### F5. H4: the preregistered wording is ambiguous (minor; reporting)
**What.** The paper said the per-dataset 80% reading is how "the preregistration words it". The wording, "C2 is labelled blob in ≥ 80% of subsamples", admits both a pooled and a per-dataset reading:
- **Pooled over the 12 preregistered datasets:** the blob rate passes.
- **Per dataset:** 10 of 12 reach 80%.

**Fix.** The paper now states both readings without privileging either.

### F6. Two descriptions of real clouds were inaccurate (minor; factual)
- **Random-init Llama counting control (exp02, learning 6).** The text said "one isolated point" and "centroids away from the data". The cloud has *two* isolated points, the means for character counts 1 and 2: 62 and 46 units from the median vs a median distance of 2.0. All three fitted centroids lie within 0.007 of a data point. **Fixed.**
- **R9, true/false city statements.** The text said "very different spread/shape". The RMS spread of the false statements is 1.58× that of the true ones (1.40 vs 0.89). Unequal, but "very different" overstated it. **Fixed** to "unequal spread".

### F7. Bibliography details (minor; factual)
- **`moisescu2025modular`.** The authors were listed in arXiv order, but the entry cites the ICLR 2026 version, which lists Daniels before Létourneau. **Fixed.**
- **Xiao et al.** A code comment gave the year as 2023 while the bibliography says 2024 (ICLR). **Fixed.**
- **Explorer links.** The repository was renamed to `shape-fitting-in-neural-geometry-in-llm-mechanistic-interpretability`. GitHub redirects repository URLs, but not GitHub Pages URLs, so the old explorer link in README, the paper, the model card and the explorer page returned 404 (checked with curl). **Fixed**; the new URL returns 200.

### F8. Construct validity of the H3 labels (open; already a stated limitation)
H3 compares labels of the top-3 PCA projection with descriptions that often come from other models, other projections or by-eye calls. Two examples:
- **R2, grokked embeddings.** Nanda et al. and Zhong et al. show circles in Fourier-frequency planes or in pairs of PCs, one circle per key frequency. A top-3 view mixes two frequencies, so the 1D expectation is approximate (the top-3 variance is 32%).
- **R9, true/false statements.** Marks & Tegmark report the separation in the top-2 PCs of LLaMA-2.

**Status.** The paper's limitation "Literature labels as ground truth" covers this. H3's n = 12, with 8 of 12 expected labels being "curve", leaves little power to beat either the permutation null or the constant baseline. No change.

### F9. Held-out residuals reuse the full cloud's PCA frame (minor)
The 5-fold held-out residual fits shapes on 80% of the points. The PCA normalisation, however, uses all points, the held-out ones included. This mild leakage affects every system's held-out residual equally. It is not used by any hypothesis test; only the preregistered helix rule uses it as a comparison between two forced fits. No change; recorded here.

**Substantive corrections** (catalog, README, paper, exp02):
- **Bhalla et al.'s geometry labels.** "Line" for temperature and age and "helix" for years are labels in their dataset table. Their own 3D PCA views show bent curves (an arch for temperature, a hook for age) and a broad cloud for years. A years panel and a geography panel do exist (the catalog said "unknown"). This does not change H3, which is coarse, but it bears on the fine-level comparison:
  - the method's *ringing* for temperature matches the arch in Bhalla's own plot;

- **Engels et al.'s layer pairing.** Fig. 4 shows Mistral-7B layer 30 for weekdays and Llama-3-8B layer 5 for months. App. Fig. 18 shows both tasks at all layers of both models, so the layer-5 weekday cloud (R5a) is covered.

## Recommendations (not done here: they need model runs or new design decisions)
1. Store each system's predicted parameters per test cloud, so that metrics (for example a finer Chamfer grid) can be recomputed without the model.
2. Evaluate Chamfer on a finer grid, or with the analytic distance to the parametric surface. Report it per coarse class.
3. Generate future test sets with several chunks per family × cell in every subset that baselines use, so that a stratified cluster bootstrap is possible there too.
4. For H3-style comparisons, take the expected labels from the source's own top-3 PCA view where one exists, not from summary tables. Preregister that choice.
